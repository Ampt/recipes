## Ingredients

- 375 grams warm water (105–110°F)
- 5 grams active dry yeast
- 7 grams honey (or 4 g sugar)
- 500 grams bread flour (all-purpose works too)
- 10 grams fine salt
- 5 grams olive oil, for the bowl

## Steps

1. Bloom the yeast: In the mixer bowl, stir 5 grams active dry yeast and 7 grams honey (or 4 g sugar) into 375 grams warm water (105–110°F). Let it sit until foamy.
2. Mix and rest: Add 500 grams bread flour (all-purpose works too). Mix on low with the dough hook for about 2 minutes until no dry flour remains. Cover and let it rest so the flour hydrates for 15 minutes.
3. Knead: Sprinkle in 10 grams fine salt and knead on medium-low (speed 2 on a KitchenAid) for 6–8 minutes, until the dough is smooth, slightly tacky, and pulls away from the sides. It will be softer than pizza dough. That's what you want.
4. First rise: Transfer to a bowl greased with 5 grams olive oil, cover, and let rise in a warm spot until roughly doubled, about 1½–2 hours (90 minutes).
5. Shape: Turn the dough onto a lightly floured counter. Fold the edges into the center all the way around, flip it seam-side down, and drag it toward you a few times to tighten the surface into a round ball. Set it on a sheet of parchment.
6. Proof and preheat: Cover loosely and let it proof while you put the Dutch oven (lid on) in the oven and preheat to 475°F. Let the pot heat for the full time. A hot pot is what makes the crust.
7. Score and bake covered: Dust the top with flour and slash it once or twice about ½ inch deep with a sharp knife or razor. Carefully lift the loaf by the parchment into the hot pot, cover, and bake.
8. Bake uncovered: Remove the lid and bake until deep golden brown and the internal temperature reads 205–210°F.
9. Cool: Cool on a rack before slicing. The inside is still setting, and cutting it hot makes it gummy.

## Notes

A same-day crusty loaf with a crackly crust and open, chewy crumb. Great for dunking in a creamy wild rice soup. For more flavor, after shaping, cover the loaf and refrigerate it overnight (8–24 hours), then bake it straight from the fridge with no extra proof. If you use all-purpose flour, hold back about 15 g of the water because the dough will be slacker. If your pot's knob isn't rated for 475°F, swap it for a metal one or cover the knob with foil.
