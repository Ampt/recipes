## Ingredients

- 375 grams warm water (105–110°F)
- 5 grams active dry yeast
- 7 grams honey (or 4 g sugar)
- 500 grams bread flour (all-purpose works too)
- 10 grams fine salt
- 5 grams olive oil, for the bowl

## Steps

1. Bloom the yeast (10 min): In the mixer bowl, stir 5 grams active dry yeast and 7 grams honey (or 4 g sugar) into 375 grams warm water (105–110°F). Let it sit for 10 minutes, until foamy. If there's no foam at all, the yeast is dead. Start over with fresh yeast.
2. Mix and rest (15 min): Add 500 grams bread flour (all-purpose works too). Mix on low with the dough hook for about 2 minutes, until no dry flour remains. Cover the bowl and let it rest for 15 minutes so the flour hydrates.
3. Knead (7 min): Sprinkle in 10 grams fine salt and knead on medium-low (speed 2 on a KitchenAid) for 7 minutes, until the dough is smooth, slightly tacky, and pulling away from the sides of the bowl. It will be softer than pizza dough. That's what you want.
4. First rise (1½–2 hr): Transfer the dough to a bowl greased with 5 grams olive oil, cover, and let it rise in a warm spot for 1½–2 hours, until roughly doubled in size. Give it more time if it hasn't doubled yet.
5. Shape: Turn the dough onto a lightly floured counter. Fold the edges into the center all the way around, flip it seam-side down, and drag it toward you a few times to tighten the surface into a round ball. Set it on a sheet of parchment.
6. Proof and preheat (45 min): Immediately after shaping, put the empty Dutch oven (lid on) in the oven and turn it on to 475°F. Cover the loaf loosely with a towel or plastic wrap and let it proof for 45 minutes. Leave the pot heating for that whole 45 minutes, not just until the oven says it's at temperature. Cast iron heats much more slowly than the oven air, and a thoroughly hot pot is what gives you a crisp crust and good rise. The loaf is ready when it looks puffy and a gentle fingertip poke springs back slowly and leaves a slight dent. In a cool kitchen this can take up to an hour. That's fine, since the pot just keeps heating.
7. Score and bake covered (25 min): Dust the top of the loaf with flour and slash it once or twice, about ½ inch deep, with a sharp knife or razor. Using oven mitts, take the hot pot out and remove the lid, lower the loaf in by the parchment, put the lid back on, and return the pot to the oven. Bake covered for 25 minutes.
8. Bake uncovered (15–20 min): Remove the lid and bake uncovered for another 15–20 minutes, until the crust is deep golden brown and the internal temperature reads 205–210°F.
9. Cool (45 min): Lift the loaf out by the parchment and cool on a rack for at least 45 minutes before slicing. The inside is still setting, and cutting it hot makes it gummy.

## Notes

A same-day crusty loaf with a crackly crust and open, chewy crumb. Great for dunking in a creamy wild rice soup. About 4½ hours start to finish, mostly hands-off. For more flavor, after shaping, cover the loaf and refrigerate it overnight (8–24 hours). The next day, preheat the pot for 45 minutes as in step 6, then bake the loaf straight from the fridge with no extra proof. If you use all-purpose flour, hold back about 15 g of the water because the dough will be slacker. If your pot's knob isn't rated for 475°F, swap it for a metal one or cover the knob with foil.
